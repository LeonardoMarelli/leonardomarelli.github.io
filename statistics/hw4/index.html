Penetration Score: LLN and CLT

Sums of i.i.d. increments, simulated for n systems per attacker and m attackers.

1. Model

  • Bernoulli: Xi ∈ {0,1}, P(Xi = 1) = p. Mean p, variance p(1−p).
  • Rademacher: Xi ∈ {−1,+1}, each with probability 1/2. Mean 0, variance 1.
  • Quantities: Sn = Σ Xi, Sn/n, Sn/√n.
  • Reading: each trajectory is one attacker probing n systems in sequence; m attackers are m independent trajectories.

2. Simulation

For n = 10, 100, 1000, 10000 the program simulates m trajectories and reports, for each quantity, the simulated value next to its theoretical value (columns marked "theory"). P(|Sn/n − μ| > 0.05) is compared with Chebyshev's bound σ²/(nε²). KS distance is the largest gap between the empirical CDF of the standardised total and N(0,1).

3. Aggregate over attackers

Here n = 100 systems per attacker. A = Σj=1..m Sn(j) is the total score of m attackers. Its variance is mnσ². Dividing by mn gives the per-system average, whose variance is σ²/(mn).

4. What the numbers show

Law of large numbers

  • Sn/n concentrates on μ: the variance of Sn/n is σ²/n, and it goes to 0.
  • P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) falls to 0 as n grows. Chebyshev's bound σ²/(nε²) is always above the simulated value, so it is valid but loose.

Variance under each normalisation

  • Var(Sn) = nσ²: grows with n.
  • Var(Sn/n) = σ²/n: shrinks with n. Averages become more precise.
  • Var(Sn/√n) = σ²: constant. Dividing by √n keeps the spread fixed.
  • For Bernoulli, E[Sn/√n] = p√n: the mean drifts upward, so only the centred version (Sn−np)/√n has a fixed centre.

Central limit theorem (Rademacher)

  • The KS distance to N(0,1) falls as n grows. At n = 10 the values are discrete, so the gap is large.
  • At n = 10000 the remaining gap is at the level of simulation noise, about 1/√m.

Systems and attackers

  • More systems per attacker (n) makes the normalised score more predictable, but the total score grows like n, so the raw total is less informative.
  • More attackers (m) multiplies the aggregate mean by m and its variance by m. The per-system average has variance σ²/(mn): it gets sharper as attackers are added.
  • For a single attacker the score is random even for large n: only the per-system average is stable.
